Hi everyone. Sorry for posting mostly about the game lately, but I’m getting deeper into developing the AI engine for my game CTOR — and it has become one of the most exciting projects of my life.

In 2017, Google DeepMind completed AlphaGo — the first program to confidently defeat the World Champion in Go. Google invested hundreds of millions of dollars into AlphaGo, AlphaGo Zero, and AlphaZero through research, infrastructure, and massive compute. What comes next?

Why CTOR is the Reincarnation of Go for AI

AlphaGo turned Go into a key environment for deep learning and hybrid neural architectures. Today, CTOR occupies a similar niche — but for a new generation of abstract games focused on environment construction, stable structural dynamics, and intuition‑driven collective behavior.

Simple rules → complex behavior

CTOR is built on four basic operations: Put, Move, Landing, Eating.  
Their combinations within a single turn create an exponential “explosion” of possibilities and a rapidly expanding state space with emergent structures. This makes CTOR a natural laboratory for studying complex systems — just as Go once was.

Deeper strategic space than classical games

In Go, depth comes from territorial battles through local operations.  
In CTOR, the same territorial struggle emerges from the interaction of local operations (Put, Move), a global operation (Landing), and a recursive transformation of the position (Eating), implemented as a cellular automaton (CA).

An ideal environment for AI training

Go catalyzed RL, policy/value networks, and MCTS.  
CTOR requires even more hybrid approaches:
• multi‑step planning  
• prediction of emergent patterns  
• RL + supervised learning hybrids  
• models of collective behavior  

CTOR — a game of collective intuition

Go is a game of individual mastery.  
CTOR enables research into:
• collective decision‑making  
• strategy alignment  
• distributed reasoning  

This opens a new domain: Collective Intelligence Games (CIG).

Strong potential for ML competitions

CTOR supports tasks such as:
• move prediction  
• position evaluation  
• outcome prediction  
• strategy clustering  
• multi‑agent RL  
• modeling collective behavior  

This makes CTOR an ideal platform for Kaggle, NeurIPS, AAAI, and IJCAI — global centers where new generations of AI developers learn and grow.

Bottom line

CTOR is the ideal game for the era of collective AI, emergent systems, and hybrid models.  
It is a research platform that may become the next global standard for AI competitions and scientific experimentation.
